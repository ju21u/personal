About

I study how satellite and airborne remote sensing can be used to detect, quantify, and understand methane emissions — work that sits at the intersection of atmospheric science, remote sensing, and environmental data analysis. I completed my Ph.D. in Environmental Science and Engineering at Harvard University (with a secondary field in Computational Science and Engineering), and hold two Bachelor of Science degrees from MIT in Civil and Environmental Engineering and in Earth, Atmospheric, and Planetary Science.

I am based in Munich, Germany, where I work with Professor Jia Chen at the Technical University of Munich on validating MethaneSAT data and estimating urban methane fluxes for Munich. I also hold appointments as a Lecturer at The Joint Graduate School of Energy and Environment in Bangkok, and as a Research Associate at Harvard University, where I continue working with Professor Steven Wofsy on the MethaneAIR and MethaneSAT missions.

Outside of research, I'm a certified dog trainer (KPA CTP).

Research

MethaneSAT & MUCCnet

Technical University of Munich · with Prof. Jia Chen · 2024–Present

  • Validating MethaneSAT satellite data against MUCCnet ground-based measurements
  • Applying geostatistical inverse modeling to estimate methane fluxes over Munich
  • Supported by the Integrated Carbon Observation System – Cities

MethaneAIR & MethaneSAT

Harvard University · with Prof. Steven Wofsy · 2019–Present

  • Developed and operationalized algorithms for Level 4 data products
  • Used Large-Eddy Simulations to quantify methane point sources
  • Collaborated with the Brandt Group (Stanford) on controlled release experiments
  • Applied geostatistical inverse modeling to estimate methane fluxes for MethaneAIR targets

Earlier research includes undergraduate work on soil moisture, climate projections in China, indoor air quality in Indian slums, low-cost particle sensors in Hawaii, and Southeast Asian haze modeling.
